# Toxic Foods

Several common human foods are dangerous or deadly for pets. Chocolate contains theobromine, which dogs and cats cannot process well; dark and baking chocolate are the most toxic, and even small amounts can cause vomiting, tremors, a racing heart, or seizures.

Grapes and raisins can cause sudden kidney failure in dogs, and the toxic dose is unpredictable, so no amount is considered safe. Onions, garlic, leeks, and chives damage red blood cells and can lead to anemia, whether raw, cooked, or powdered.

Xylitol, an artificial sweetener found in sugar-free gum, candy, some peanut butters, and baked goods, is extremely toxic to dogs. It triggers a rapid drop in blood sugar and can cause liver failure very quickly. Lilies are especially dangerous to cats: even brushing against the pollen and grooming it off can cause fatal kidney failure.

If your pet eats any of these, treat it as an emergency. Call a vet immediately, or contact an animal poison control line, even if your pet seems fine at first. Have the product packaging ready so you can tell them roughly how much was eaten and when. Do not try to make your pet vomit unless a professional tells you to.
