# Health

Puppies and kittens need a series of core vaccinations, usually starting around six to eight weeks of age and repeated every three to four weeks until about sixteen weeks. This series builds immunity against serious diseases like parvovirus, distemper, and rabies. Your vet will set the exact schedule and booster timing.

Adult pets should have a wellness check-up at least once a year. Senior pets, generally dogs and cats over about seven years old, benefit from twice-yearly exams, since problems like arthritis, kidney disease, and dental disease become more common and are easier to manage when caught early.

Parasite prevention is year-round work. Most pets need regular flea and tick control and monthly heartworm prevention, along with periodic deworming. Your vet can recommend the right products for your pet's species, weight, and local risks.

Give any prescribed medication exactly as directed, for the full course, even if your pet seems better. Stopping early can let an infection return or worsen. Finally, watch for red-flag symptoms that need prompt veterinary attention: difficulty breathing, repeated vomiting or diarrhea, collapse, seizures, a swollen or painful belly, inability to urinate, or sudden lethargy. When in doubt, call your vet.
