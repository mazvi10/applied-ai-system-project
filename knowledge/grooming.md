# Grooming

Brushing frequency depends on coat type. Short-coated dogs and cats can usually be brushed once a week, while long-haired or double-coated breeds need brushing several times a week, or even daily, to prevent painful mats and reduce shedding. Regular brushing also spreads natural oils and lets you spot lumps, parasites, or skin problems early.

Most dogs only need a bath every four to six weeks, or when they are visibly dirty. Bathing too often strips the coat of protective oils and can dry out the skin. Always use a pet-formulated shampoo, since human products have the wrong pH. Most cats groom themselves and rarely need baths unless they get into something.

Nails should be trimmed regularly, roughly every three to four weeks for most pets. If you can hear nails clicking on the floor, they are too long. Trim only the tip to avoid cutting the sensitive quick, and use treats to keep the experience positive.

Dental care is easy to overlook but important. Brushing your pet's teeth several times a week with pet toothpaste, along with dental chews and regular veterinary cleanings, helps prevent painful gum disease and tooth loss. Never use human toothpaste, which can be toxic to pets.
