# Exercise

Exercise needs vary a lot by breed, age, and health. High-energy working and sporting breeds such as border collies, huskies, and retrievers often need one to two hours of vigorous activity a day, while many toy breeds and flat-faced breeds are content with shorter, gentler walks. Older dogs still need daily movement to stay mobile, but at a lower intensity and with more rest.

For puppies, a common guideline is the "five minutes per month of age" rule: about five minutes of structured exercise per month of age, once or twice a day. So a four-month-old puppy would get roughly twenty minutes at a time. This protects growing joints from overexertion while still burning energy.

Cats need exercise too, mostly through play. Short bursts of chasing, pouncing, and climbing several times a day keep indoor cats fit and satisfy their hunting instincts. Wand toys, balls, and climbing trees all help.

Enrichment matters as much as physical exercise. Puzzle feeders, scent games, training sessions, and rotating toys keep the mind engaged and prevent boredom-related behaviors like chewing or excessive barking. A tired, mentally satisfied pet is usually a well-behaved one.
