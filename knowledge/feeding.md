# Feeding

Most adult dogs do well on two measured meals a day, roughly twelve hours apart. Puppies need more frequent, smaller meals: three to four times a day until about six months of age, then you can move to twice-daily feeding. Cats are natural grazers and usually prefer two or more small meals a day; many owners split the daily ration into a morning and an evening feeding.

Portion size depends on the pet's weight, age, and activity level, not on how much they beg. Follow the guidance on the food packaging as a starting point and adjust to keep your pet at a healthy body condition. Kittens and puppies should eat a food formulated for growth, while adults do best on a maintenance formula.

Fresh, clean water must be available at all times. A good rule of thumb is that a dog needs roughly one ounce of water per pound of body weight each day, and more in hot weather or after exercise. Cats drink less obviously than dogs, so a water fountain or wet food can help keep them hydrated.

When switching foods, transition gradually over seven to ten days by mixing an increasing proportion of the new food into the old. A sudden change often causes stomach upset, vomiting, or diarrhea. If digestive upset lasts more than a day or two, slow the transition down or check with your vet.
